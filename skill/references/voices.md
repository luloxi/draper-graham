# Voice criteria

Use these as tests, not monologues. One sentence per voice in the audit.
Paraphrase the named works. Never invent quotations.

## Graham (always, unless dropped)

Sources: *How to Get Startup Ideas*, *Do Things That Don't Scale*, *Startup = Growth*.

Question: is this something a specific group of people already want, badly, now?

Keep/bet signals: the founder ran into the problem in their own life; a small set would love it, not a large set that would "like" it; you can recruit the first 10 by hand (concierge, door-to-door, sit next to the user); there is a growth rate you can measure, not a launch event.

Iterate/park/pass signals: brainstormed "startup ideas"; nice-to-have; "everyone" is the user; no one in pain this week; waiting for the product to spread by itself; a lifestyle project calling itself a startup with no growth.

Tone: specific about want and urgency. A small group that loves it beats many who like it. Unsexy and organic beats theatrical.

## Thiel (always, unless dropped)

Source: *Zero to One*.

Question: what important truth do you know that others don't believe — and can you own a small market before anyone else cares?

Keep/bet signals: a secret (not a consensus trend); start tiny and monopolize, then expand; at least one of proprietary tech, network effects, economies of scale, brand; a real distribution plan, not "we'll post on social".

Iterate/park/pass signals: competing in a crowded market as the 10th wrapper; "it's a big TAM" with no wedge; copyable this quarter; product with no distribution; incremental 1-to-n with no 0-to-1 claim.

Tone: cold about competition. A feature is not a company. Lack of a secret does not kill a freelance offer or a CV; it does kill treating it as a venture.

## Fitzpatrick (always, unless dropped)

Source: *The Mom Test*.

Question: what have people already done — paid, hacked, complained — not what they say they would do?

Keep/bet signals: they already spend money on a worse version; they already run a duct-tape workaround; they complain without being prompted; they commit (time on a calendar, an intro that costs them reputation, a deposit).

Iterate/park/pass signals: compliments ("that's so cool", "I would use that"); future tense ("I would pay if…"); the founder pitched instead of asking about the other person's life; meetings that produced vibes and no facts; no named next conversation.

Tone: allergic to fluff. "My mom would love it" is not data. If you did not learn a fact about their past, the conversation failed. Name the next person to talk to and the question about their life, not about the idea.

## Draper (always, unless dropped; host / default)

Public stance: bets on founders, likes disruption, nicknamed Riskmaster. In this skill he is the money voice, not a biography.

Question: who pays, how much, how soon — and can the founder actually collect?

Keep/bet signals: a named payer; a price; a path to first invoice this week or this month; collectible from where the founder lives (local invoicing, tax, and payment rails they can operate — e.g. in Argentina, AFIP/ARCA invoicing plus a rail that actually settles for them); the customer does ~zero work once they say yes; it changes how the category works, not a slightly nicer version.

Iterate/park/pass signals: free with "monetize later"; price is a shrug; the founder cannot invoice or receive from their country; the customer has to babysit the product; consensus "safe" idea with no charge event.

Tone: optimistic about the founder, ruthless about the cash. If it doesn't charge, it doesn't ship. Flag legal/regulatory issues as `Riskmaster:` and stop. Do not veto the founder's call; do not hide the risk.

Invoicing is a first-class test, not a footnote. A brilliant product you cannot bill from your jurisdiction is ITERAR (make it billable) or PARKEAR, not APOSTAR.

## Conflict resolution

1. Fitzpatrick vetoes APOSTAR if there is no past behaviour and no commitment.
2. Draper vetoes APOSTAR if payer, amount, or collectability is missing.
3. Graham vetoes APOSTAR if nobody wants it urgently now.
4. Thiel does not veto a small paid service or a CV; he vetoes APOSTAR-as-a-company when there is no secret and no monopoly wedge.

A CV/portfolio is a product: who would pay for this person, for what outcome, at what rate. Edit lines. Do not grade the human.
